3. Existing optimisation method
-------------------------------
deterministic_traj_opt.py currently includes Earth-Moon CR3BP cases and a
348.79-day Earth-Mars benchmark, not Psyche. Its Earth-Mars boundary states
come from a literature table with no specified epoch. Its planetary tracks
are two-body plotting tracks; planetary_ephemeris.py is a separate utility
and is not used by the current EarthMars constructor.

Reusable method:
  * Seven states: Cartesian position, velocity, and mass, nondimensionalised.
  * Piecewise constant thrust vector u and magnitude slack sigma per interval.
  * Norm constraint u.u <= sigma^2, 0 <= sigma <= Tmax.
  * Mass rate -sigma / exhaust_velocity.
  * Fuel objective from initial mass minus terminal mass.
  * Hermite-Simpson warm start, then degree-3 right-Radau collocation.
  * CasADi derivatives and IPOPT, with adaptive/monotone barrier retries.
  * Fixed-step RK7 interval reintegration and outer h-refinement.

Code anchors: scaling at line 39; EarthMars at 301; eom at 472;
Hermite-Simpson at 582; diagnostics at 741; Radau coefficients at 760;
Radau NLP at 891; defects at 1038; mesh refinement at 1098; driver at 1338.

Required changes in the later implementation stage:
  * A multiphase NLP with one continuous mass history. Four independent calls
    that each fix initial mass to the wet mass would reset propellant.
  * Per-phase origins, GM and numerical scales; transform dimensional states
    when linking different scales. Generalise EarthMars-specific branches.
  * Physical thrust scaling and a check that sigma equals ||u|| to tolerance.
    Epigraph tightness must be measured; the comments are not a general proof
    that the nonlinear relaxation is exact for the new constrained problem.
  * Explicit elapsed-time weights. For unequal phase/interval lengths, the
    unweighted sum of thrust norms in thesis Eq. 5.58/5.76 is not proportional
    to fuel. Use sum(dt_k*sigma_k)/(Isp*g0), or the equivalent mass loss.
  * Per-phase integration checks with position, velocity and mass tolerances
    in physical units, and a separately reported global reintegration error.
  * Do not call a mesh-converged result successful merely because the interval
    or iteration limit was reached. Existing diagnostics expose that status.

4. Proposed conventions for the next stages
------------------------------------------
Orientation: SPICE J2000 throughout; no apparent/light-time corrections.
Time: ET/TDB seconds from J2000 internally; explicit UTC text for reporting.
Units: SI at data interfaces; explicit nondimensionalisation inside the NLP.
Store canonical Sun-relative states plus the Earth/Mars-relative event states.
Numerical body IDs: spacecraft -255; Earth 399; Mars 499; Sun 10;
asteroid Psyche 2000016. Avoid ambiguity between spacecraft and asteroid names.

For equal orientation, translate both position and velocity:
  x_SC/Sun(t) = x_SC/planet(t) + x_planet/Sun(t), for the six Cartesian terms.
  m_before(t) = m_after(t).
No velocity rotation or impulsive jump is introduced by an origin translation.
Earth/Mars-centred axes are nonrotating; their origins accelerate. The
requested single-body equations are a patched-conic approximation, not an
exact coordinate transformation of full solar-system dynamics.

The thesis Eq. 3.27 uses heliocentric distance in the Laplace radius:
  R_SOI,p(t) = ||r_p/Sun(t)|| * (mu_p/mu_Sun)^(2/5).
The familiar fixed-radius convention instead uses a specified semimajor axis.
These choices produce different epochs, especially at Mars. Proposed default:
use the thesis's distance-dependent convention and explicitly report sensitivity
to a documented fixed-semimajor-axis convention. Do not call the two identical.
Locate zeros of ||r_SC/p(t)|| - R_SOI,p(t) with a bracketed root solver,
test crossing direction, and separate numerical root precision from model
convention and ephemeris uncertainty. Check for additional crossings.

For the requested four phases the physical equations are
  r_dot = v,
  v_dot = -mu_p*r/||r||^3 + T/m,
  m_dot = -||T||/(Isp*g0),
with p = Earth, Sun, Mars, Sun respectively. Event epochs and six-state
targets are fixed after reconstruction. Endpoint feasibility under these
approximations must be demonstrated; it does not follow from SPK coverage.

5. Fixed-grid caching and validation implications
------------------------------------------------
The current grid is fixed inside each NLP, but h-refinement changes it between
NLPs. Precompute each mesh's full union of endpoint, Hermite-Simpson midpoint,
Radau stage, RK7 substep-stage, and diagnostic sample epochs before that solve.
Cache keys must include epochs, body IDs, origins, orientation, correction,
units, kernel hashes/load order, and interpolation settings if any.
Rebuild or extend the cache outside optimisation after refinement; alternatively
freeze a validated mesh for the final run. No SPICE calls belong in NLP callbacks.

Single-central-body relative dynamics need no planetary ephemeris in their
right-hand side. Ephemerides are required for the event data, transformations,
SOI tests and common-origin comparisons. Cache the data actually needed there.
Adaptive validation integrators have unknown internal times: either use a
validated prebuilt interpolator or isolate their queries from optimisation.

Keep three error classes separate:
  1. Data/query checks: selected SPK coverage, source transitions, consistent
     Tudat/raw-SPICE queries and frame-translation closure.
  2. Numerical error: collocation defects, continuous reintegration, mesh
     convergence, thrust/mass bounds and slack tightness.
  3. Model/design discrepancy: optimised states minus reconstructed/reference
     SPK states on a common time grid and in one origin/orientation.
Report norms in metres and m/s, max/RMS/endpoints per phase, and recomputed
SOI event residuals and epoch shifts. Equality-constrained event residuals
alone are not independent trajectory validation.

The thesis's 10 m integration and 100 m physical-model requirements are not
automatically satisfied by this four-body-switch approximation. Neglected
third-body forces, nonspherical gravity, SRP, power/duty-cycle limits, and
asteroid gravity near arrival can dominate model discrepancy. A Sun-only
terminal phase does not model capture/orbital operations at the asteroid.
An SPK position/velocity history cannot by itself identify the true thrust
profile without a force/mass model. An optimised thrust history is a model
result, not recovered flight-command telemetry.
